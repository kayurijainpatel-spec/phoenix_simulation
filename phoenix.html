The Phoenix Program is Washington's answer to the Tet Offensive: a systematic campaign to identify, capture, defect, or neutralize the Viet Cong civilian infrastructure hidden among the population. OPERATIONAL REVIEW
Historical Context
Between 1965 and 1972, the Phoenix Program reported neutralizing approximately 81,000 members of the Viet Cong Infrastructure — roughly 26,000 killed. Critics including former CIA officers raised persistent questions about accuracy. William Colby later acknowledged before Congress that many "neutralizations" were of incorrectly identified suspects.

Operation Wandering Soul (Ghost Tape 610) exploited Vietnamese beliefs that souls killed far from home, and not properly buried, wander in torment. Its tactical effectiveness was disputed. Its effect on civilians who had no way to know the voices were a recording was never measured.
For Discussion
01What was the significance of this simulation? What did you take away, and what do you think you were meant to take away?
02Command gave you metrics. Those metrics shaped every decision. Who designed them — and who was accountable for what they produced?
03Operation Wandering Soul weaponized grief. Is there an ethical limit to psychological warfare? Who decides where it is?
04Phoenix was largely hidden from the American public. How does secrecy change what a government is willing to do?
05Were the people who carried out Phoenix — operatives, PRU soldiers, interpreters — morally responsible? What about the people who designed the system?
06The U.S. later applied Phoenix-style models in Iraq and Afghanistan. What does that continuity tell us?
